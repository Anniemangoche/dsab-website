Caregivers

Communicating with a child or adult with Down syndrome

Practical ways to understand and be understood, with or without spoken words.

What you will learn

  • Use clear, simple language without talking down
  • Support speech with gestures, pictures and objects
  • Give time to respond

Many people with Down syndrome understand far more than they can say. Speech can take longer to develop, and sounds may be harder to form. That does not mean there is nothing to say.

Ways to help

  • Face the person, get their attention, then speak
  • Use short sentences and one idea at a time
  • Add gestures, pictures or real objects
  • Wait. Count to ten silently before repeating
  • Repeat back what you understood so they know they were heard

Respect

Speak to adults as adults. Use their name, ask their opinion, and let them make choices about their own lives wherever possible.
